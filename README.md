# Alderwick — Medieval Town Simulator

Working Godot project: `game/project.godot`. Launch with **Play Town.cmd**; **Open in Godot.cmd** opens the editor. The working source and the earlier `live-preview` snapshot are separate so editing does not require closing the preview.

## Controls

- Start in the overhead view. Tab or the view selector cycles first person, overhead, and third person.
- Overhead: drag to orbit and scroll to zoom.
- First/third person: WASD/arrows move, mouse looks, Shift runs, Space jumps.
- Esc releases the mouse. Click in the game to capture it again.
- F talks to a nearby resident. In overhead view, click a resident. A right-side panel accepts freely typed messages and keeps a scrolling conversation history. Enter sends; Shift+Enter adds a line. Replies are generated by the local AI and spoken by a consistent local neural voice; the Character voices checkbox mutes or restores speech.
- Player appearance · choose a face opens the scrollable skin selector. Each distinct base model appears once. Select a face and Use its appearance to change the player and see the result in third person. The choice is saved locally. Resident names and jobs are unaffected.
- E opens/closes a nearby door or uses a chair/bed. E or Space gets up.
- The Visit a place menu moves the player to the square, district entrances, South gate watch, Carpenter workyard, Farm deliveries, or Western market for easier previewing. Paths also connect the districts for walking.
- R returns to the main street. M mutes/restores the soft music, normally -34 dB.
- N or the checkbox enables the optional day/night cycle. Off on launch; the preview cycle lasts 60 seconds. Longer speeds remain available.

## Current world

The connected world includes the original town, downloaded buildings, and the western village with its additional homes. The additional architecture includes Feira Castle, Castle XIX, Viking buildings, a forge, a watchtower, a fortress, and Burg Arnstein outside the old town. A shared ground body extends beneath the districts so they can be reached on foot.

The two scanned château ruins were removed from the project at user request on 4 November 2026. The main castle district and Burg Arnstein remain. Ada’s delivery and Ansel’s survey now use the western market and western gatehouse.

The fourteen added enterable buildings use physical interiors in their actual world positions. Walkable variants of the imported meshes have real doorway openings; the castles use their stone courtyards/keep space. The earlier underground copied rooms and doorway teleportation are no longer used. The forge retains its original workshop geometry and equipment, with an extra outdoor workbench for the downloaded tools. The original 13 modular houses also use detailed Quaternius furniture. Upper floors of the original houses and the closed facades in the scanned western village are not accessible; the four added western homes are enterable.

Forty-five residents use local routines: six work indoors, four vendors staff produce stalls, and thirty-five have outdoor jobs and routines. There are 31 residents in the main city, eight in the western village, and six in the castle districts. The cast mixes 24 residents using the newer knight, mercenary, night hunter, and Viking models with 21 using the original four villager models. The newer characters have 22-bone skeletons and eleven animation clips each. Their original downloads are preserved; editable fitted Blender files are in `source-assets/rigged-characters`.

Fourteen residents have fixed daytime guard posts. This includes every shield-bearing resident: six knights and seven Vikings, plus one additional castle guard. They guard the town gates, crossings, town hall, granary, storehouse, training yard, western entrances, and three castle approaches. Guards remain at their posts during the day, can speak with passing residents or the player, go home to beds at night, and return to the same posts in the morning. A guard never abandons a post to walk to a conversation.

Farmers harvest carrots and carry visible crates to district markets. Delivered crates accumulate beside the stalls and update the market's stock sign. Carpenters use four workbenches and produce visible wooden stools. Couriers carry supply crates from stores to the workyards. A two-handed carry pose is applied over the walking animation, keeping hands on the crate while the legs continue moving. Job completions become personal memories that residents can discuss.

Residents invite someone within walking distance to a clear meeting spot. One waits while the partner walks over; only conversation partners turn to face each other. Up to five pairs can converse in four alternating turns, with timing that waits for spoken audio when it is audible. Interruptions, timeouts, and nightfall release both partners. Other residents retain their walking, work, stall, or resting direction. Conversations use personal work, actual recent deliveries and completed furniture, and news learned from partners. Market sellers alternate arranging goods and serving at the stall.

The nine animated animals are two cows, two horses, two pigs, two sheep, and a small white dog. The dog target height is 30 cm. Animal calls use positional sound with distance attenuation. A static resting cat decorates the town; the static wildlife collection remains available in the assets and is not presented as additional animated species.

At night, residents head indoors, occupy reserved beds, and close doors after arrivals. The town has 77 usable beds, with capacity considered when assigning homes. Residents give each other space in the streets and use one-person-at-a-time door queues. Morning residents first walk toward the exit, then queue at the actual doorway, so a distant resident cannot reserve the door while blocking roommates. Furniture occupancy prevents the player and residents sharing one seat or bed.

Castle XIX has a physical passage through its stone gate into the courtyard. Furniture leaves that aisle clear. Interior routes use a finer grid for narrow gaps between beds and tables. The street floor query recognizes the two raised bridges, so eastern residents can use the crossings rather than taking a long route around the river.

## Animation corrections

The transferred runner clips now animate foot positions together with the legs and reset all bone channels. The jump's lateral torso tilt is removed. A separate neutral idle is used for the player. The knight's shield and spear and the Viking's shield use rigid bone attachments, with clearance from the body; carried equipment is hidden while sleeping.

## Content and future work

Character speech runs locally using eight Kokoro neural voice profiles, with a consistent profile per resident. The bundled runtime starts automatically; it does not require a paid speech account or send dialogue over the internet. Each spoken turn comes from the resident's position, becomes quieter with distance, and fades out by 26 metres. The listener follows the player, including in third person. In overhead direct conversations it moves near the selected resident so zooming out does not silence the reply. Background music remains very soft at -34 dB and ducks further during direct conversations. Runtime credits and notices are listed in `VOICE-CREDITS.md`.

Conversations now use local Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct inference, individual character backgrounds, actual game context, and saved per-character chat history. Nearby NPC exchanges also request generated turns. The active player chat has no canned topic replies. Character backgrounds are in `CHARACTER-STORIES.md`; these are narrative context rather than saved conversations. Movement, jobs, schedules and physical transactions remain controlled by the local simulation. Live microphone input and AI control of physical actions remain future work. See `LOCAL-AI.md` and `CHARACTER-VOICE-PLAN.md`.

The face selector uses nine approved existing base models, with a single card per base and actual Godot-rendered portraits. The earlier batch of 45 resident images is retained under `game/portraits`, but repeated residents do not create extra player-skin choices. Additional adult models and six prepared story designs remain staged for visual review; see `PLAYER-APPEARANCES.md` and `STAGED-CHARACTER-STORIES.json`.

Downloaded tools and curios are placed as decorations and marked with future trade IDs. Trading, inventory, and mini-mission transactions are not implemented yet.

Sources and embedded author/license metadata are recorded in `DOWNLOADED-ASSET-CREDITS.md` and `.json`. Quaternius Medieval Village MegaKit, Fantasy Props MegaKit Standard, and Universal Animation Libraries Standard supply the modular town, detailed furniture, and daily motions. Medieval Village & Dungeon Kit 0.3.0 supplies the second group of buildings. Animal audio credits are in `game/audio/animals/CREDITS.md`. The background MP3 is user-supplied.

## Graphics quality

The town menu offers Performance, Balanced, High and Ultra, with an FPS indicator. The choice is saved in user://graphics-settings.json. Performance and Balanced reduce internal 3D resolution and distant detail and use interpolated 30 Hz physics. High and Ultra use native 3D resolution and 60 Hz physics. Repeated static building parts are batched without changing collisions or door interactions. Distant resident animation and close-range foot correction are reduced while schedules continue. Available RAM is used as needed; there is no artificial low memory cap. Quality does not guarantee a particular frame rate on integrated graphics.
